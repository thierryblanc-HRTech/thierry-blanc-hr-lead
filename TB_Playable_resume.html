2. Admin-HR — HR Portal (Service Delivery & Exception Management)

Exception-based dashboard: pending/processing/expiring documents, riders to validate, contractless workers, pending employer documents — HR works a queue of exceptions instead of searching case by case.

Document governance: 6 mandatory document types (ID, driving license, IBAN, health insurance, employee file, vehicle file) and 7 optional types (work-permit request, criminal record, proof of residence, etc.), each independently configurable.

State-machine employee file: Pending → Validated/Rejected (with reason shown to the employee) → Verified → Archived; same pattern reused for engagement status (Pending → Verified → ReadyToWork → Suspended → Ended).

Cross-system integration: Ops data (shift attendance, scheduling status) surfaced directly on the HR profile — one screen instead of two systems.

Service delivery: integrated ticketing (Jira Service Management) with FAQ self-service; 10,578 self-service views vs. only 116 tickets escalated to a human in 12 months.

Measured outcome: 1,200 employees managed, zero compliance flags across state audits, 4.75/5 satisfaction sustained through a union relations crisis.

3. GTA — Time & Attendance Engine (Compliance Automation)

ETL pipeline: pulls schedules/clock-ins from the scheduling tool by department in 14–30 day chunks; anti-ghost-data filtering; pagination up to 2,000 pages; exponential-backoff retry on rate-limit errors.

Reconciliation logic: matches a clock-in to its shift by ID or by a 2h/14h time-proximity window; generates a virtual punch when a paid absence has no clock-in; merges orphan punches into a hybrid record.

Legal-rules engine: vacation rights computed per segment (age, activity rate, profile change) — 20/25 base days, ×1.4 factor for 7-day rotations, pro-rata reduction rules for sickness/pregnancy/unpaid leave; rest-time, span and break-length compliance checked automatically against Swiss labour law thresholds; cross-border telework capped and tracked with automatic renewal alerts.

Hour-balance engine: separates structural gap (employer under-scheduled) from employee-caused shortfall — protects both compliance and fairness.

Resilience: self-relaunching job that checkpoints its own progress before a runtime timeout, then resumes automatically; batched writes (500 rows) with duplicate-proof keys; forced text formatting to avoid spreadsheet date-conversion bugs on long durations.

4. SODF & BridgeGTA — Offboarding, Legal Documents & Architecture Discipline

Legal termination engine: computes the legally protected termination date, applying statutory protection-period rules (illness/accident, pregnancy) with day-count precision, and suspends notice periods automatically when protection applies.

Severance/social-plan calculator: computes indemnity from seniority, age and dependents; includes a controlled manual-override workflow with full audit trail for edge cases.

Official forms: auto-generates statutory employer certificates (domestic and cross-border versions) and unemployment-office annexes, with conditional field-mapping for special cases (partial pay, meal allowance averaging).

Historical payslip reconstruction: multi-year salary-account rebuild across a legacy and a current payroll system, with pagination and translation handling.

Interconnection layer (BridgeGTA): exposes narrow, purpose-built services between GTA and SODF instead of duplicating data — each module keeps one clear source of truth.

Security posture: zero hard-coded secrets (OAuth2 tokens/API keys held in a secure properties store), token caching, and an anti-crash safeguard that refuses to overwrite the employee database if an API call returns a suspiciously small dataset.

External validation: reviewed by an independent Microsoft Dynamics audit firm (DBE — Dynamics Business Excellence SA, Dec. 2024), which confirmed the architecture and attributed the platform build directly to me by name.

5. Smood_legal — Mistral-Powered Forensic HR Data Platform

A standalone project demonstrating daily, hands-on AI integration in HR — beyond tool awareness. Built end-to-end in Python, from ingestion to LLM-classified evidence and a Flask review interface, for a live HR legal case involving ~14,000 emails, ~3,600 chat messages, ~526 Jira tickets and ~838 documents.

Multi-source ingestion pipeline: 4 raw sources (eml, chats, Jira CSV, documents with OCR) normalised to JSONL, loaded into a DuckDB forensic database with typed schemas and idempotent re-runs.

LLM classification at scale (Mistral Small): every item (email, chat topic, Jira ticket, document) is qualified by closed-vocabulary prompts — actual level exercised, legal qualification, legal basis, evidence type, party impact (charge/discharge/mixed/neutral). Two-pass + double-check pattern with token accounting per run.

Time & attendance reconstruction: a parallel pipeline ingests TimeMoto exports, computes a strict median (9h56), audits abnormal days with an LLM rule engine (R1–R5), and produces a 903-day calendar with a full audit trail per day.

Legal costing engine: 3 juridical scenarios (contract clause valid / invalidated / mixed statutory), computing overtime and statutory working-time premiums year by year, with a 60h/year threshold (LTr art. 13 al. 1) and Swiss case law applied (ATF 4A_337/2024).

Delivery: Flask SPA with 8 tabs, a native PDF generator (fpdf2) producing juridical-grade timesheets, and a job runner with a clean stop-flag for LLM batches.
